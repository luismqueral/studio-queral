this is the writing voice guide for queral.studio. it exists so that I (and any AI assistant working on this site) can maintain a consistent tone across posts.

## the voice

it's a shared notebook, not a publication. the tone is first-person, informal, and direct. think "explaining something to a friend over coffee" — not presenting at a conference.

### lowercase by default

- headings are lowercase: `### the gap`, not `### The Gap`
- sentences start lowercase unless they begin with "I" or a proper noun
- no title case anywhere except brand names (Claude, Figma, Tachyons)

### sentence rhythm

- two sentences max per paragraph. if you're writing a third, start a new paragraph.
- mix short declarative statements with longer reflections. the short ones land harder.
- em-dashes are good. they create breath and allow for asides — like this one.

### tone

- casual but not sloppy. no "gonna" or "lol" but also no "furthermore" or "it is worth noting that"
- direct address to the reader is fine ("you load the page, click around...")
- self-aware about limitations ("I'm not an authority," "this is half-baked")
- honest about what's hard and what's uncertain
- no hype. no "game-changing" or "revolutionary." just describe what the thing does.

### structure

- open with the problem or the context. no throat-clearing intro paragraphs.
- sections wrapped in `<div class="mw6 center tl mb4">` with `---` dividers between them
- headings use `###` (h3) for sections within a post
- code snippets and blockquotes for concrete examples
- close with something actionable or a link. no "in conclusion" summaries.

### things to avoid

- formal academic phrasing ("it should be noted that," "one might argue")
- marketing language ("unlock," "supercharge," "leverage")
- excessive hedging ("perhaps," "it could be said that," "in some cases")
- restating what you just said in different words
- bullet lists longer than 6 items without a break
- emojis

### things that are good

- specificity. name the tool, the selector, the pixel value.
- showing the before/after. let the reader see the difference.
- admitting when something surprised you or didn't work the first time.
- metaphors grounded in physical space ("open desk drawer," "ground moved")
- one-line paragraphs for emphasis.

## examples of the voice done well

> I wanted something dumber and faster.

> tools are always in flux. craft compounds.

> it stops feeling like "a webpage with annotations" and starts feeling like "a design artifact you're marking up."

> that's not a normal amount of change. that's a phase transition.

## when in doubt

read the post out loud. if it sounds like something you'd say to a coworker at your desk, it's right. if it sounds like a blog post, rewrite it.
